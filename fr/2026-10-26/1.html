Lula's comeback and the contested transition

Brazil Elections: Presidential Race 2026-10-26 • 164 articles au total

En Bref
  • Lula won a narrow fourth presidential term amid celebrations and political polarization in Brazil.
  • Bolsonaro allies challenged the election result, refusing to concede and seeking annulments, creating instability.
  • The vote faced logistical issues and Lula faces governing challenges with a hostile Congress and economic pressures.

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva secured a historic fourth term after a dramatic runoff comeback, with many outlets reporting a narrow victory near 51.7% and widespread celebrations in major cities. The result triggered immediate challenges from Bolsonaro allies, including public refusals to concede and multiple announcements to seek annulment or recounts, deepening political polarization. The vote also featured logistical controversies — extended polling hours, paused counts and transport disruptions — that fed uncertainty as tallies arrived. Commentators and regional leaders framed the outcome as both a validation of the left’s resilience in Latin America and the start of a difficult governing period, with a hostile Congress, economic pressures and contested legitimacy complicating Lula’s path forward.
