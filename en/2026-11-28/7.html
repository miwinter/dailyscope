Costa Rica bars UN rapporteur, sparking diplomatic dispute

Costa Rica UN Diplomacy 2026-11-28 • 15 articles in total

In brief
  • Costa Rica denied UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese entry, citing national security and alleged terrorism links, sparking international criticism.
  • The UN, Amnesty International, and diplomats urged Costa Rica to respect UN immunities and academic freedom and reverse the ban.
  • Albanese rejected the allegations, demanded proof or apology, and received an honorary doctorate remotely, highlighting tensions over UN oversight of Palestinian territories.

Costa Rica denied entry to UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese, citing a national security alert and alleged links to terrorism, a move that drew international scrutiny. The United Nations, Amnesty International and other diplomatic actors urged the government to reverse the ban and stressed UN immunities and freedom of academic and civic exchange. Albanese condemned the decision as an "insult" to the UN, demanded proof or a retraction of the accusations, and was nevertheless awarded an honorary doctorate via videoconference from an airport. The incident has amplified tensions over external oversight of the Palestinian territories and raised broader questions about Costa Rica’s engagement with UN human rights mechanisms.
