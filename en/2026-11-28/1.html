Great Power Rivalry: Military Signalling, Influence, and Resource Competition

Great Power Rivalry 2026-11-28 • 26 articles in total

In brief
  • Great power rivalry involves military signaling, covert operations, and economic competition affecting global and regional security landscapes.
  • Russia's advanced naval forces and hybrid attacks complicate NATO deterrence, increasing risks of escalation and requiring resilience against multifaceted threats.
  • Economic and political contests in Africa and regional flashpoints like Taiwan demonstrate how great powers influence local stability and impact long-term strategic capacities.

The great power rivalry centers on a mix of hard military signalling, covert and hybrid operations, and economic competition for strategic resources and influence. Russia’s modernization of strategic forces and growing sabotage and maritime threats force NATO and regional partners to reassess deterrence and preparedness. The United States and China compete economically and politically across Africa and other regions, while political rhetoric and diplomatic framing shape alliance cohesion and public perception. Regional flashpoints from Taiwan to the Middle East show how great power involvement amplifies local instability and complicates conflict resolution. Economic strains—defense spending priorities and energy challenges—will shape each power’s capacity to sustain long-term competition.
