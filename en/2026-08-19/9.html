Court orders Imran Khan moved to hospital amid health concerns

Imran Khan Detention Health 2026-08-19 • 10 articles in total

In brief
  • Pakistan's Supreme Court ordered former PM Imran Khan moved from prison to hospital due to health concerns.
  • Khan's condition reportedly worsened, prompting urgent medical calls and a rare visit from his sister after nine months.
  • The transfer is framed as both a humanitarian act and a judicial step amid ongoing political and legal conflicts.

Pakistan's Supreme Court has ordered jailed former prime minister Imran Khan be moved from prison to a hospital amid mounting concerns about his health. Indian reports add that Khan's sister visited him after nine months and supporters and lawyers say his condition has deteriorated, prompting urgent calls for medical care. Regional and international outlets frame the ruling as a judicial intervention to allow medical checks and monitoring by doctors while noting the move occurs amid ongoing legal and political battles. Khan's party and family have pressed for the transfer, and coverage highlights both the humanitarian and high‑stakes political dimensions of his detention.

Countries covering this topic

Indian coverage: urgency, family access and deterioration

Indian sources emphasize the perceived deterioration of Khan's health and the immediacy of moving him to a hospital, highlighting his sister's first visit in nine months. Reporting frames the Supreme Court order as a response to urgent medical concerns and as part of the broader political‑judicial dispute around his detention.

Regional/international reporting: judicial order and medical checks

Regional and international outlets present the Supreme Court decision as a procedural step to ensure medical evaluation and monitoring, stressing the court's intervention and doctors' assessments. Coverage is more neutral in tone, situating the transfer within ongoing legal proceedings and political tensions but focusing on the health‑care and custodial oversight aspects.
