Harvard settles over mishandled donated body parts

Harvard Body Parts Settlement 2026-08-19 • 6 articles in total

In brief
  • Harvard University settled multimillion-dollar lawsuits over a former morgue manager selling donated human body parts on the black market.
  • The settlement resolved multiple civil claims and regulatory scrutiny without a trial, with reported amounts ranging from $53 million to $90 million.
  • The case raised ethical concerns about donor consent, respect for remains, and oversight of body donation programs, prompting calls for reform.

Several international reports cover a multimillion-dollar settlement by Harvard University to resolve lawsuits over mishandled and sold donated human body parts. Coverage highlights allegations that a former morgue manager sold cadaver tissues on the black market, prompting civil claims from donors' families and regulatory scrutiny. Reported settlement figures vary across outlets — commonly cited amounts include $53 million, $68 million and $90 million — but all accounts say the payments resolve the litigation without a trial. The case has sparked debate about donor consent, respect for remains, and the oversight of body donation and research programs. Harvard maintains it acted in good faith while the controversy has renewed calls for reform in medical-institution practices.
