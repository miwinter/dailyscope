Influence and military signaling in great power competition

Great Power Competition 2026-08-19 • 14 articles in total

In brief
  • Russia employs influence operations and military signaling amid internal consolidation, prompting Western and regional defensive measures.
  • The U.S. rearmament and security reviews respond to evolving global threats and past strategic failures.
  • Regional shifts include Japan's military growth and North Korea-Russia ties, complicating Asian security dynamics.

Recent reports show a multipronged great-power contest combining influence operations, military signaling, and strategic realignments. Western and regional actors warn of Kremlin-directed propaganda and covert interference campaigns even as Moscow projects force through drills and alleged secret drone bases, a posture some analysts read as signaling weakness amid internal consolidation. Washington is responding with rearmament measures, security reviews and reflection on past withdrawals, while Asia sees rising activity from Japan’s tests and organizational expansion and a deepening North Korea–Russia alignment. Observers stress that modern power is harder to translate into decisive wins, and that careful assessments are needed to avoid over- or under-reacting to perceived threats.

Kremlin influence and interference concerns

European and Western sources emphasize active Russian influence operations aimed at shaping politics abroad, from cultural centers and disinformation to alleged targeted interference against French figures. They call for investigations, monitoring and defensive responses to protect democratic processes and counter propaganda.

Russian military signaling and internal consolidation

Intelligence and regional coverage highlight Moscow’s outward military moves—covert drone bases and Baltic force displays—while domestic purges suggest leadership consolidation that may drive aggressive signaling. Analysts debate whether these actions represent genuine capability expansion or compensatory displays masking vulnerability.

U.S. rearmament and security posture reassessed

U.S.-focused reporting frames recent actions as a mix of remediation and deterrence: replenishing missile stocks, reviewing institutional ties to foreign actors, and reassessing strategic failures such as the Afghanistan withdrawal. The perspective stresses restoring credible capabilities and tightening homeland security measures amid evolving threats.

Japan’s expanding military footprint in Asia

Coverage from the region flags Japan’s stepped-up testing and potential Defense Ministry expansion as indicators of growing military normalization. Commentators, including Chinese experts, view the moves as signs of remilitarization with implications for regional stability and defense postures.

Arctic and Greenland assessments

Analysts focusing on the Arctic caution against alarmism, arguing Russia currently lacks intent to seize territories like Greenland and that strategic priorities differ from sensational claims. The view urges measured appraisal of Arctic competition and its limits.

The evolving limits of superpower warfare

Analytical commentary argues that modern economic, technological and political constraints mean superpowers struggle to achieve rapid, decisive victories. The perspective reframes great-power competition as more complex, prolonged and costly than traditional models imply.

North Korea–Russia alignment and regional leverage

Reporting on Pyongyang’s ties with Moscow presents the partnership as a source of money, military cooperation and diplomatic leverage that could increase pressure on the United States and destabilize regional security dynamics. Observers warn the alignment complicates deterrence and proliferation concerns in East Asia.
