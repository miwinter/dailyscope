US Army Secretary Driscoll Resigns Amid Pentagon Tensions

US Army Secretary Resigns 2026-09-01 • 17 articles in total

In brief
  • Dan Driscoll resigned as U.S. Secretary of the Army amid clashes with adviser Pete Hegseth and ties to Vice President J.D. Vance's faction.
  • The White House framed the resignation as routine personnel turnover after 18 months, giving minimal public explanation.
  • The resignation is seen as part of broader leadership turmoil and political friction affecting U.S. defense policy and readiness.

Dan Driscoll has resigned as U.S. Secretary of the Army, a move various outlets link to months of clashes inside the Pentagon, particularly with adviser Pete Hegseth. The White House presented the departure as a routine personnel change after 18 months in office, offering minimal public explanation. Other reports place the resignation in a wider pattern of senior leadership churn and political friction tied to appointees aligned with Vice President J.D. Vance, raising questions about policy direction and readiness. International coverage diverges between straightforward announcements and narratives framing the exit as symptomatic of deeper turmoil in U.S. defense leadership.
