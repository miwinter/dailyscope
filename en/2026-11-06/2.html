Israel coalition talks

Israel Coalition Talks 2026-11-06 • 32 articles in total

In brief
  • No clear majority after Israeli elections, with former military chief Gadi Eisenkot gaining key Arab party support and leading coalition talks.
  • Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc lost crucial allies, reducing parliamentary strength amidst disputed results and rising tensions.
  • President Isaac Herzog begins consultations, likely nominating Eisenkot to form a fragile, possibly minority government reliant on cross-bloc agreements.

After elections left no clear majority, Israeli parties are engaged in intense coalition talks with former military chief Gadi Eisenkot emerging as a leading candidate supported by Arab lists while Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc has lost key allies. President Isaac Herzog has begun consultations and is set to nominate a lawmaker to try to form a government, a decision that will shape the next phase of negotiations. Observers warn the likely outcome is a fragile, possibly minority government dependent on cross-bloc agreements and Arab party backing. The process is marked by uncertainty, accusations from Netanyahu about the vote, and significant policy and regional implications depending on the eventual coalition makeup.

Eisenkot's ascent and Arab backing

Multiple reports indicate Gadi Eisenkot has secured endorsements and crucial backing from Arab parties, putting him ahead in recommendations to President Herzog and positioning him as the frontrunner to assemble a government. While some outlets treat this as a parliamentary majority or near-majority, others note that Eisenkot still faces political and practical hurdles to convert endorsements into a stable coalition.

Presidential consultations and nomination timetable

President Isaac Herzog has started consultations with party leaders and signalled he will name a candidate—expected on Sunday or November 8—to attempt to form a government, emphasizing the need for creativity and boldness. Several outlets outline the constitutional steps and the likely handover of the first mandate to the leading challenger, while tracking shifting recommendation tallies among lawmakers.

Coalition math, obstacles and regional considerations

Analysts underscore the fragile arithmetic in the Knesset, the prospect of a minority government supported by Arab lists, and the practical obstacles—portfolio bargains, abstentions, and local vote blocs such as Bedouin voters—that could complicate coalition formation. Reports warn that even with endorsements, forming a durable government will be difficult and could have broader implications for domestic policy and regional diplomacy.
