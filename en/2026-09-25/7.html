Suicide attempts aboard USS Abraham Lincoln prompt reviews of mental health and welfare

US Military Mental Health 2026-09-25 • 5 articles in total

In brief
  • Eight suicide attempts occurred aboard USS Abraham Lincoln during a prolonged deployment, prompting investigations and mental health reviews.
  • Officials and lawmakers, including the Navy secretary, are addressing crew welfare and safety protocols amid operational and Iran-related tensions.
  • Media reports highlight public scrutiny and calls for systemic changes to improve military welfare and prevent future incidents.

Five international reports describe eight suicide attempts aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln during a long, record-setting deployment. The incidents have prompted investigations and reviews of mental health support, safety protocols, and welfare procedures by naval officials and lawmakers, with the Navy secretary testifying to Congress. Some outlets connect the events to the strain of extended deployments and elevated operational tensions, including reporting that situates the incidents alongside the Iran-related conflict. No public accounts in these pieces indicate fatalities, though they highlight serious concerns about crew morale, readiness, and access to support.
