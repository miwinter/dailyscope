Escalation, diplomacy and wider fallout from the Russia–Ukraine war

Russo-Ukrainian War 2026-09-25 • 50 articles in total

In brief
  • Moscow offers to negotiate peace on its terms but rejects current ceasefire proposals while continuing military operations.
  • Kyiv urges sustained international pressure, proposes limited ceasefires, and relies on foreign volunteers and new weapons.
  • NATO and European states warn of Russian hybrid threats, improve defenses, and intelligence warns of possible strikes on Europe.

Coverage shows a multifaceted conflict: Moscow reiterates readiness to negotiate a “durable” peace while rejecting a ceasefire, even as Kyiv presses for continued international pressure and seeks specific pauses such as an energy ceasefire. NATO and European governments warn of expanded Russian hybrid and kinetic threats to allies and are bolstering readiness, while intelligence agencies publicly caution that Russia could target Western European countries. Fighting continues on and beyond Ukraine’s front lines with multiple strikes, counterstrikes and reports of expanding reach, producing fresh civilian casualties and dire humanitarian testimony. The war’s effects extend globally through disrupted grain exports and debates over private-sector technologies such as Starlink being used to support Ukrainian operations, and analysts are watching diplomatic channels involving major powers for possible negotiated outcomes.
