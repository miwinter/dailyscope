Global South Multipolar Strategy

Global South Multipolar Talks 2026-11-21 • 5 articles in total

In brief
  • Global South leaders push for a multipolar order via regional cooperation and multilateral dialogue shaped by diverse national strategies.
  • Divergent UN votes and regional alliances reveal tensions between strategic autonomy, solidarity, and external influences within Global South states.
  • External actors like the UAE and Saudi Arabia influence regional dynamics, complicating efforts toward unified Global South-led cooperation.

A summit in Cape Town and parallel diplomatic moves across regions indicate a concerted push by Global South actors to shape a more multipolar international order through expanded regional cooperation and multilateral dialogue. While leaders articulate shared priorities on trade, security, and development, actual alignments remain heterogeneous: some states emphasize unity and resistance to Western pressure, others pursue bilateral ties or external investment. Divergent UN votes, summit declarations, and Gulf engagement in the Indian Ocean show the interplay of strategic autonomy, regional solidarity, and outside influence. Overall, the trend is toward greater agency for Global South states, but the path to a cohesive multipolar order will be uneven and contested.

Ibero‑American solidarity and anti‑blockade stance

Cuba’s reaffirmation of Iberian‑American cooperation and its denunciation of the U.S. blockade underscore a strand of Global South politics that prioritizes regional solidarity and resistance to Western sanctions. Such rhetoric seeks to solidify collective responses to external pressure and promote South‑South collaboration.

Gulf investment and influence in the Indian Ocean

The UAE’s rise as the dominant Arab investor in the Comoros demonstrates how external state actors are reshaping regional influence through targeted diplomacy and economic engagement. That competition—here between UAE and Saudi interests—adds a layer of strategic contestation to efforts at building an independent multipolar order.
