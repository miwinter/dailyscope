TikTok COPPA settlement and U.S. enforcement

TikTok COPPA Settlement 2026-08-22 • 11 articles in total

In brief
  • U.S. authorities reached a roughly $400 million resolution with TikTok over alleged unlawful data collection from children under 13 without parental consent.
  • The case involves DOJ enforcement of COPPA, requires privacy safeguards for minors, and sets a significant legal precedent for child-data protection.
  • Reports vary on the settlement amount and whether it was a court ruling or negotiated settlement, highlighting its impact on future social media regulations.

U.S. authorities reached a major resolution with TikTok over allegations it collected data from children under 13 without parental consent under COPPA, with most outlets reporting a roughly $400 million payout and commitments to strengthen protections for minors. Reports emphasize DOJ involvement, required changes to how under‑13 accounts and data are handled, and ongoing oversight to monitor compliance. A few outlets present inconsistent figures — one cites a multi‑billion‑dollar amount and another $559 million — while another frames the outcome as a court loss rather than a negotiated settlement. The case is being portrayed as a significant precedent in U.S. child‑data enforcement and could prompt broader changes across social media platforms.
