Colombia's security offensive and rising violence

Colombia Security Push 2026-08-10 • 7 articles in total

In brief
  • Colombia's new government launched military operations killing armed group members including a dissident FARC commander.
  • Bombing attacks on the president's inauguration day killed and wounded police, showing ongoing security threats.
  • Civil society warns that violence and harsh security measures threaten peace and demand better protection and accountability.

Colombia's new administration has launched a security offensive in the Amazon and other regions, with authorities reporting that operations killed six members of armed groups, including a dissident FARC commander. Military strikes and intelligence-led operations have been framed as early, decisive actions against dissident networks. At the same time, bombing attacks on the president's first day in office killed at least one police officer and wounded others, underlining immediate security challenges. Civil society and international observers warn that the broader pattern of violence — including dozens of social leaders killed in recent months — raises serious concerns about protection, accountability, and the impact of a tougher security approach on peace efforts.
