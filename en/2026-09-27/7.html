Stalled diplomacy over the Strait of Hormuz

US–Iran Diplomacy 2026-09-27 • 21 articles in total

In brief
  • President Trump rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and a ceasefire, maintaining a hardline stance.
  • Iran offered a conditional plan to reopen the Strait within seven days, urging the U.S. to respond and preferring negotiation with firm nuclear redlines.
  • International actors expressed concern over U.S. pressure and rising tensions, highlighting geopolitical divisions and risks to regional security.

A run of international reports says U.S. President Donald Trump publicly rejected Iran’s offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and a proposed ceasefire, underscoring a hardline U.S. posture. Tehran had proposed a conditional plan — reportedly to reopen the chokepoint within seven days if Washington met its terms — and said the ball was now in the U.S. court. Observers warn the impasse heightens risks to Gulf shipping and regional security, with related incidents such as Houthi attacks cited as complicating factors. Third countries and institutions voiced concern about rising tensions: China criticized U.S. pressure at the UN while other actors portrayed the situation as diplomatically fragile and polarized.
