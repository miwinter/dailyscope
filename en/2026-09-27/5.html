Standoff Over Hormuz: Iran’s Seven‑Day Offer and Global Responses

Iran Diplomacy Watch 2026-09-27 • 59 articles in total

In brief
  • Iran proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if the U.S. meets conditions, offering concessions to restart talks.
  • The U.S. rejected Iran's offers, maintaining a hardline stance and coordinating regional pressure measures against Tehran.
  • China and others call for diplomacy and cautious talks continue despite heightened tensions and Iranian warnings of military retaliation.

Iran proposed a conditional plan — including a seven‑day timeline to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and concessions on nuclear matters — framing the move as a route back to negotiations. The United States, led publicly by President Trump, rejected Tehran’s offers, reinforcing a hardline stance while some U.S. officials framed pressure and isolation as having effect. Regional measures such as air‑space restrictions and allied coordination have escalated tensions, prompting Iranian warnings of retaliation and public assertions of military readiness. China and other third parties urged diplomacy and expressed concern about sovereignty, and limited back‑channel contacts and phased‑deal talks signalled cautious diplomatic openings amid high risk of further escalation.
