Shifting Diplomatic Currents: SCO Momentum, Russia–West Frictions and Regional Dialogues

World Leaders Dialogue 2026-09-02 • 105 articles in total

In brief
  • China and Russia use the SCO and bilateral meetings to push a multipolar agenda and explore trilateral diplomacy with the US.
  • Western nations focus on defense and deterrence against Russia while maintaining selective dialogue through forums like the G20.
  • Regional actors advocate for climate action, diplomatic solutions, multilateral reform, and managing geopolitical tensions amid ongoing conflicts.

Recent coverage shows a two-track diplomatic landscape: China and Russia are using the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation and high-level bilateral meetings to push a multipolar agenda and deepen strategic cooperation, while Western capitals focus on security responses to alleged Russian provocations and the limits of cooperation. At the same time, leaders continue to explore selective engagement—G20 and other summit-level contacts—and Moscow has signalled the possibility of trilateral talks with Washington and Beijing. Regional forums and small-state diplomacy remain active, with Pacific leaders pressing climate priorities as some attendances are uneven, and Middle Eastern and African actors advocating negotiation and multilateral reform. Across these stories, rivalry and the need for practical dialogue coexist, producing varied strategies from containment and deterrence to outreach and institutional reform.

China–Russia SCO and strategic outreach

Beijing and Moscow are framing the SCO and high-level bilateral meetings as platforms to advance a multipolar alternative to Western-led institutions, deepen strategic ties, and announce joint initiatives in areas like technology and security. Their messaging emphasizes regional leadership, proposals to embed SCO norms more widely, and the possibility of high-profile trilateral diplomacy involving major powers.
