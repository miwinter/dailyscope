Widespread wildfires, earthquakes, epidemics and humanitarian crises

Disaster Relief Operations 2026-07-25 • 111 articles in total

In brief
  • Southern Europe faces unprecedented wildfires causing mass evacuations, emergency declarations, military deployment, and EU assistance requests under extreme weather conditions.
  • Venezuela endures catastrophic earthquakes causing thousands of deaths and displacement, requiring prolonged reconstruction and international aid.
  • Multiple crises include a deadly Ebola outbreak in DRC, severe food insecurity in Gaza, conflict-driven humanitarian challenges in Sudan, coordinated rescue operations for maritime and industrial accidents, and intensified disaster preparedness and cooperation globally.

This set of reports is dominated by an unprecedented wildfire emergency across southern Europe, where France and Spain have declared states of emergency, mobilized military forces, requested EU help and evacuated hundreds of thousands as fires driven by extreme heat and wind rage across regions. Alongside the blazes, Venezuela grapples with catastrophic earthquakes that have killed thousands, left tens of thousands displaced and prompted long-term reconstruction and international aid appeals. Public-health crises continue in the Democratic Republic of the Congo with a fast-moving Ebola outbreak causing hundreds to more than a thousand deaths, while conflict-affected Sudan, the Gaza Strip and refugee camps face acute protection, nutrition and access problems that humanitarian agencies warn could worsen. The coverage also highlights dispersed rescue operations — maritime rescues, industrial and transport accidents, landslides and regional flood and storm threats — and points to growing emphasis on cross-border cooperation and preparedness exercises to improve future responses.

Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The coverage underscores an urgent need for sustained surveillance, medical resources and local engagement to contain transmission.

International agencies and on-the-ground reporting underline severe food insecurity, rising child malnutrition and hazardous sanitary conditions in Gaza, with some short-term improvements noted but repeated warnings that restrictions on aid and ongoing hostilities could rapidly reverse any gains. The perspective calls for urgent, sustained humanitarian access and funding to prevent further deterioration.
