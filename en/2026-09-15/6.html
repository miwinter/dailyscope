Supreme Court Keeps Mail‑In Voting Rules Intact

US Supreme Court Voting Rulings 2026-09-15 • 26 articles in total

In brief
  • The U.S. Supreme Court denied emergency requests to tighten mail-in voting rules, keeping existing procedures before the midterms.
  • Lower courts issued temporary blocks on restrictions, and ongoing litigation leaves room for future legal challenges.
  • Some reports focus on political disputes and other election-related legal issues like a DHS probe and alleged illegal voting.

International coverage shows the U.S. Supreme Court declined emergency requests to tighten or block mail‑in voting rules, effectively preserving existing ballot‑by‑mail procedures ahead of the midterms. Many outlets frame the rulings as a setback for President Trump and his effort to change voting rules, while noting the decisions leave room for further litigation. Several reports emphasize the temporary or last‑minute nature of the interventions, with lower‑court judges also blocking proposed restrictions. A subset of pieces highlights political rhetoric—claims of fraud and conservative dissent—while two articles focus on other election‑related legal concerns, such as a DHS probe and an isolated alleged illegal vote case.
