Bilateral momentum: security pacts, investment ties and contested deals

Region: Bilateral Deals 2026-09-20 • 146 articles in total

In brief
  • A trilateral U.S.-Denmark-Greenland security deal causes local unease and competing sovereignty claims while maintaining regional stability.
  • U.S. arms sales and security cooperation balance strategic benefits with political and supply controversies.
  • Countries pursue diverse bilateral ties including Chinese infrastructure projects, economic deals, diplomatic ceremonies, and punitive or protective foreign policies.

This set of reports shows a busy period of bilateral diplomacy spanning Arctic security, defence sales, infrastructure investment and routine ceremonial ties. A highly visible trilateral package linking the United States, Denmark and Greenland dominated coverage, provoking competing claims about control, reassurances about sovereignty, and local unease. Washington’s continued arms transfers and proposed sales — from air‑defence packages to F‑35s — underline security cooperation and political friction with regional partners. Elsewhere, China and other external powers deepen economic footprints through infrastructure, resource and tourism deals while smaller states consolidate aid, scholarships and consular exchanges. Simultaneously, several diplomatic ruptures and disputes — from Latin America to Africa and fisheries rows in the North Atlantic — remind that bilateral ties can be both cooperative and contentious.
