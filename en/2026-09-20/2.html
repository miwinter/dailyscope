AI push and press crackdown dominate coverage of the Trump presidency

US: Trump Presidency 2026-09-20 • 76 articles in total

In brief
  • Trump plans an "AI Force" and AI czar to boost U.S. AI leadership, amid skepticism about details and oversight.
  • The White House's ban on major news outlets raises concerns about press freedom and First Amendment rights internationally.
  • Reports highlight Trump family financial scrutiny linked to Russian funds and the president's tense domestic political posture.

International coverage of the Trump presidency is clustered around two dominant storylines: a presidential push to create an "AI Force" and appoint an AI czar, and a sweeping ban on major U.S. news outlets from White House access. Reporting on the AI initiative ranges from straightforward descriptions of the proposal to skepticism about details, funding, and regulatory implications, with some outlets noting branding exercises such as polls to rename AI. Coverage of the media exclusions emphasizes concerns about press freedom, constitutional questions, and the impact on transparency, with many international outlets framing the move as an escalation. Other strands of reporting highlight persistent scrutiny of Trump family finances tied to Russian-linked payments, and domestic political turbulence — shortened vacations, schedule reshuffles, midterm messaging, and rhetoric that critics say fuels division.
