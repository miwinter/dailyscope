AfD surge and CDU collapse reshape Germany's regional politics

Germany AfD Rise 2026-09-21 • 40 articles in total

In brief
  • The far-right AfD made major gains in northeastern German states, notably topping polls in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and challenging established parties in Berlin.
  • The CDU experienced historic losses under Friedrich Merz, triggering leadership debates and complicating coalition formations at federal and regional levels.
  • International observers warn these election results signal rising populism and extremism, affecting Germany’s domestic policies and its role within the EU.

Recent regional elections in Germany produced striking gains for the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD), which topped polls in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and other northeastern states even as the Left made advances in Berlin. The CDU under Friedrich Merz suffered historic setbacks, prompting warnings of a political "earthquake" and intense debate about leadership and coalition arithmetic. International commentators emphasize that these regional outcomes complicate federal coalition-building, could shift policy on immigration and security, and may influence Germany's posture within the EU. Coverage combines alarm about rising extremism and social fragmentation with analysis attributing the AfD’s rise to voter dissatisfaction with established parties and regional economic and social grievances.
