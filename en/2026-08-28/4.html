Tighter borders, local backlash and humanitarian strains

Migration: Border Policy 2026-08-28 • 56 articles in total

In brief
  • The U.S. has sharply tightened immigration controls by suspending visa processing, revoking visas, raising fees, and expanding enforcement with contested tools like electroshock gloves.
  • Europe faces violent anti-migrant protests and camp attacks amid security fears at Ceuta, highlighting conflicts between humanitarian needs and local tensions.
  • Global migration is increasingly securitized with intensified enforcement, border militarization, reduced asylum applications, and ongoing refugee crises stressing protection challenges.

News across regions show a sharp tightening of migration controls—especially U.S. moves to suspend immigrant visa processing, revoke large numbers of visas and raise fees—while courts and analysts debate legal and practical consequences. Simultaneously, U.S. enforcement practices and equipment purchases (notably electroshock gloves) have provoked political and human-rights pushback. In Europe, the Ceuta crisis has prompted violent anti-migrant protests, attacks on camps and concern over migrants’ safety, exposing strain between humanitarian needs and local security fears. Elsewhere, regional border security measures, military drills and crime-related interdictions reflect broader securitization, even as refugee crises, maritime rescues and voluntary returns underline ongoing humanitarian and displacement challenges.
