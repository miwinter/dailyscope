Southeast Asia haze: cross-border fires, health alerts and calls for accountability

Southeast Asia Haze Crisis 2026-10-18 • 6 articles in total

In brief
  • Peat and forest fires in Indonesia caused severe haze across Southeast Asia, triggering health warnings, school closures, and highest-level meteorological alerts.
  • The crisis escalated cross-border tensions with calls for accountability and legal actions against companies linked to the fires.
  • Environmental impact includes biodiversity loss and calls for regional cooperation and long-term prevention measures.

Peat and forest fires in Indonesia have produced dense haze that spread across Southeast Asia, prompting the regional meteorological agency to issue its highest-level alert and causing school closures and restrictions on outdoor activity. Cities including Singapore are among the worst affected, with residents lining up for N95 masks and authorities warning that air quality may not improve for several days. The crisis has heightened cross-border tensions, with neighbouring countries expressing frustration and Singaporean leaders weighing legal action against companies linked to the blazes. Environmental coverage highlights broader consequences for biodiversity and forest loss, while governments and health agencies coordinate emergency responses and environmental groups call for long-term prevention measures.
