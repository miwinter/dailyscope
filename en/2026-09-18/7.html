Elections and Autocracy in Putin's Russia

Russia: Democracy Erosion 2026-09-18 • 12 articles in total

In brief
  • Russia's parliamentary vote is controlled by the Kremlin to exclude opposition and legitimize policies including territorial claims.
  • Observers warn the election hides Putin's strategic aims and risks regional security amid war and international concern.
  • Historical and individual profiles show entrenched autocracy and systemic obstacles for dissent under Putin's regime.

Russia's parliamentary vote is presented by the Kremlin as a routine electoral process, but reporting and observers portray it as tightly managed to secure Kremlin control and legitimize policy goals, including contested territorial claims. Numerous outlets document coercion, candidate disqualifications, and the imprisonment of dissidents, arguing the contest lacks meaningful competition. Analysts and intelligence assessments warn that the electoral choreography masks deeper strategic aims by Vladimir Putin and carries significant regional security implications. A few pieces profile token or isolated challengers who nevertheless face structural obstacles, while historical analysis traces how initial reforms gave way to entrenched autocracy under Putin. Together the coverage frames the vote as a snapshot of Russia's long-term democratic erosion and its consequences for international relations.
