Region: Bilateral Deals 2026-09-18 • 179 articles in total

In brief
  • U.S. approval of F‑35 sales to Saudi Arabia signifies a key development in regional and global defense dynamics.
  • Multiple countries pursue expanded bilateral diplomacy through defense cooperation, economic investment, and high-level visits.
  • The emerging EU–Canada partnership reflects strategic realignment amid U.S. diplomatic pressures and global geopolitical shifts.

Recent reporting shows an intense burst of bilateral diplomacy spanning defense sales, infrastructure and resource investments, and a steady cadence of high-level visits and summits. Security and arms cooperation feature heavily — notably U.S. approvals for F‑35 sales to Saudi Arabia and recurrent public discussion of deeper U.S.–Poland military ties — while regional defence cooperation and exercises crop up across Asia, Africa and Europe. Economic deals and investment partnerships are prominent, from large Chinese and Russian interests in Guinea’s mining sector to Korean-backed Central Asia projects, port and rail upgrades, and credit lines to support Portuguese investment in Cabo Verde. Parallel diplomatic activity includes state visits, consular business, scholarships, anti-corruption pacts and migration or refugee talks, illustrating efforts to convert political alignment into concrete cooperation across trade, security and development.
