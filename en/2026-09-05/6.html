UN map reform elevates Africa's size

UN Map Diplomacy 2026-09-05 • 23 articles in total

In brief
  • The United Nations endorsed a resolution to promote world maps showing Africa at a larger scale to correct distortions in traditional projections.
  • The initiative is led by Togo, supported by the African Union, and faces opposition including from the United States, sparking debate on map projection methods.
  • Changing map projections has symbolic significance and may influence diplomatic relations and perceptions of regional power.

The United Nations has adopted a resolution to promote revised world maps that present Africa at a materially larger scale and reduce distortions produced by traditional projections. The initiative, propelled by a Togo-led campaign and backed by the African Union and many member states, aims to correct longstanding misrepresentations and influence education, media and diplomatic materials. The decision sparked a technical debate over map projections—urging a move away from Mercator toward alternatives such as Equal Earth—and recorded notable opposition, with the United States voting against the measure. Observers warn that while the update is symbolic rather than territorial, changing cartographic standards can reshape perceptions of regional weight and carry diplomatic and geopolitical consequences.
