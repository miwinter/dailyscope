AfD's historic Saxony-Anhalt victory unsettles Germany and Europe

Germany AfD Campaigns 2026-09-07 • 90 articles in total

In brief
  • The AfD achieved around 43–44% in Saxony-Anhalt, sparking coalition challenges and pressure on CDU leadership.
  • Profiles of AfD figures highlight social media influence and controversial pasts shaping voter appeal.
  • International and institutional reactions warn about far-right normalization and geopolitical risks linked to AfD's rise.

A wide range of international reports covers the Alternative for Germany’s (AfD) dramatic showing in Saxony-Anhalt, where exit polls and final counts put the party near or above 43–44 percent, though short of an outright majority. Coverage mixes straightforward result reporting with analyses of difficult coalition math, rising pressure on the CDU and speculation about consequences for national leadership and upcoming federal politics. Several pieces profile Ulrich Siegmund and other AfD figures, noting social-media reach and controversial pasts, while commentators and institutions—from EU capitals to German bishops—express alarm about the normalization of far-right positions. The campaign themes highlighted include immigration, security and economic anxiety, and many outlets warn the result could reverberate across Germany and Europe, intensifying polarization and debates over alliances and policy direction.
