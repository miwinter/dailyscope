Volcanic ash and shifting routes reshape regional air links

Region: Aviation Connectivity 2026-09-07 • 17 articles in total

In brief
  • Volcanic ash from Anak Krakatau caused major airport closures and flight disruptions in Indonesia, impacting tens of thousands of passengers.
  • Commercial fleet changes and new airline routes are reshaping regional connectivity and tourism in the Asia-Pacific.
  • Airports manage operational surges and pricing strategies to maintain travel resilience amid natural and economic challenges.

A series of Anak Krakatau eruptions has produced ash clouds that forced Jakarta’s main airport and several other Indonesian airports to close, cancelling and delaying hundreds of flights and affecting tens of thousands of passengers. Multiple international outlets reported ongoing monitoring, extended closures and widespread rerouting as authorities and airlines prioritized safety over schedules. Separately, Pacific island tourism authorities warned that airline fleet changes — notably Solomon Airlines returning an aircraft to its lessor — could weaken international connectivity and pressure island tourism. Other reports highlight positive connectivity developments such as a new direct Mumbai–Hà Nội route, while operational and economic pieces discuss surge management at transit hubs and shifting ticket prices on key routes. Together the items show how natural hazards, commercial fleet decisions and new route openings are rapidly altering regional accessibility and travel resilience.
