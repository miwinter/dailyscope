Great Power Rivalry: US, Russia, China

Great Power Rivalry 2026-11-18 • 27 articles in total

In brief
  • The U.S., Russia, and China compete to shape the post-war order through diplomacy, military pressure, and influence operations, risking regional polarization and conflict.
  • Moscow and Beijing promote a coordinated alternative world order narrative while cautiously avoiding direct conflict, especially over Ukraine and Western alliances.
  • Russia expands military and political influence globally; the U.S. counters with alliances and diplomacy; Indo-Pacific tensions over economy and security raise crisis risks.

The coverage depicts an intensifying three‑way contest in which the United States, Russia and China vie to shape the post‑war international order through summit diplomacy, regional pressure and influence operations. Beijing and Moscow project growing coordination and competing narratives that challenge Western institutions, while Russia exerts military and political pressure across Europe and its neighbourhood. The United States counters with alliance building, diplomatic engagement in Asia and warnings about strategic competition over technology, minerals and security ties in the Global South. These dynamics raise risks of regional polarization, proxy competition and miscalculation—particularly around nuclear posture, critical‑minerals supply chains, and contested border zones.

Summits, optics and trilateral diplomacy

High‑level meetings among Trump, Putin and Xi and the surrounding diplomacy dominate the rivalry’s public face, shaping expectations about possible alignments or de‑escalatory outcomes. Symbolic imagery and summit politics can obscure substantive frictions over trade, technology and influence even as leaders test each other’s intentions.

Russia–China strategic narrative and hedging

Moscow and Beijing advance a shared narrative of an alternative world order and emphasize coordination while managing the risks of open confrontation. China’s cautious posture on Ukraine and its critique of Western groupings leave room for partnership with Russia without provoking direct conflict with the United States.

European security: pressure, messaging and resistance

Russia’s military actions, political messaging and influence campaigns—from drones near Moldova to attempts to reshape European politics—have triggered alarm across NATO and EU circles and calls for firmness. European analysts and former diplomats stress deterrence, alliance cohesion and resistance to negotiated arrangements that would legitimize Russian gains.

Competition in the Global South, Indian Ocean and Central Asia

Russia’s expanding security ties and presence—from Madagascar to the Mozambique Channel and Central Asia—seek footholds far from Europe and spark U.S. and French concern about strategic access. Smaller states and regional organizations become arenas where great‑power contestation over basing, maritime control and security partnerships plays out.

Indo‑Pacific strategic competition: resources, deterrence and diplomacy

The Indo‑Pacific rivalry blends economic leverage and military risk—tensions between Japan and China, U.S. promises to boost rare‑earth production, and debate about China’s nuclear trajectory all underscore multiple pressure points. These issues complicate alliance management and raise the stakes for crisis stability in the region.
