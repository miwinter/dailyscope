Vučić and Serbia's fraught pre-election moment

Serbia Elections: Vučić 2026-10-23 • 15 articles in total

In brief
  • Serbia's election features mass student mobilization, intimidation, cyberattacks, and heavy campaigns as President Vučić aims to strengthen power.
  • Media highlight Vučić's narrative blaming foreign interference and discuss the election's risks to democracy and regional stability.
  • Observers debate whether the vote will end Vučić's dominance, noting strong government rallies and deep political polarization.

Serbia's approaching vote is shaped by a tense mix of mass student mobilization, accusations of intimidation and cyberattacks, and heavy-handed campaign tactics as President Aleksandar Vučić seeks to consolidate power. Domestic commentators question whether Vučić's move from the presidency into the election arena signals a break from long-standing personal rule or a reinforcement of it, while explainers highlight institutional stakes and international observation. Large pro-government rallies, polls commissioned by the administration, and promises of higher pensions compete with street occupations and hundreds of opposition student candidates. Vučić frames challenges as foreign-directed attacks, an argument that feeds polarization even as analysts abroad assess whether the election could mark the end of his dominance. Observers warn that violence, threats, and deep polarization could undermine public trust in the result and regional stability.
