El Salvador–Haiti counter-gang operation

El Salvador Haiti Counter-Gang 2026-11-11 • 9 articles in total

In brief
  • El Salvadoran forces destroyed gang properties and coordinated with Haitian authorities in a cross-border counter-gang operation in Haiti.
  • President Bukele framed the strikes as decisive and praised by some regional leaders, but reports cite about 20 casualties and raise concerns.
  • The US expressed concern over the operation, highlighting tensions in regional security cooperation and questions about legal and humanitarian impacts.

El Salvadoran forces conducted coordinated operations in Haiti that destroyed gang properties, including the house of Lanmò San Jou and a gang headquarters, documented in photos and videos. President Nayib Bukele publicly framed the strikes as a hardline, cross‑border counter‑gang campaign carried out with Haitian authorities’ cooperation and praised by some regional leaders. Media footage and government statements have been accompanied by reports of casualties (one outlet cites about 20 dead), prompting questions about the operation’s scale and humanitarian and legal implications. The US State Department has reportedly expressed concern about El Salvador’s actions, underscoring tensions in regional security cooperation.

Cross‑border counter‑gang action and its fallout

Multiple outlets emphasize the operational coordination and visual record of the mission — photos and videos showing Salvadoran troops destroying gang houses and headquarters in Haiti, and Haitian authorities reportedly cooperating. Salvadoran sources and President Bukele present the strikes as decisive, large‑scale measures to curb gangs, while other reports note casualties and raise questions about proportionality and accountability. The US has reportedly questioned El Salvador’s approach, reflecting diplomatic unease even as some regional leaders praise the intervention.
