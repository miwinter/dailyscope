Pasdaran-linked refrigerator hacks in Europe

Iran Cyber Attacks Europe 2026-11-10 • 19 articles in total

In brief
  • French authorities accuse Iran's IRGC-linked cyber units of crippling about 700 European supermarkets' refrigeration, causing spoilage, equipment failures, and fires.
  • France and allies vow retaliation and EU sanctions amid ongoing investigations into IoT vulnerabilities exploited by the attackers.
  • Attribution is politically sensitive and complex; experts call for careful forensic analysis due to growing cyber threats to civilian infrastructure.

French authorities have attributed a widespread cyber campaign that sabotaged supermarket refrigeration systems across Europe to Iran, specifically naming IRGC-linked cyber units. The alleged operation affected hundreds of stores—reports cite roughly 700 sites—causing food spoilage, equipment failures and some fire incidents, and prompting diplomatic protests and threats of EU sanctions. Paris and partner governments have vowed retaliation while investigators and CERT-FR/ANSSI document technical indicators and note exploitation of IoT and controller vulnerabilities. Attribution remains politically charged and technically challenging, with Iran denying responsibility and experts warning of broader risks to supply chains.

French accusations and diplomatic response

France has publicly blamed Iran and its Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps for the refrigerator hacks, summoning Tehran's envoy and warning of further measures including possible EU sanctions. Several allied governments and officials echoed Paris's stance and pledged coordinated responses while investigators continue to collect evidence.

Scale and impact on supermarkets and supply chains

Authorities report the campaign struck hundreds of supermarkets—commonly cited as about 700 sites—leading to spoiled goods, economic losses and isolated fires that underline risks to food safety and logistics. Incident reports and media coverage emphasize the operational disruption to refrigeration networks and the potential knock‑on effects for supply chains and consumer confidence.

Technical attribution, Pasdaran units and uncertainties

Investigations and security reporting point to IRGC-linked cyber units such as Shahid Kaveh (and Shahid Shoostari in some technical reports) and highlight exploitation of vulnerable IoT and refrigeration controllers, but independent, conclusive attribution remains difficult. Experts urge coordinated forensic analysis and caution about politicized claims while noting the growing use of state‑linked cyber operations against civilian infrastructure.
