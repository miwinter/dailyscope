Spain calls for multilateral diplomacy

Global Peace Diplomacy 2026-11-20 • 20 articles in total

In brief
  • Spain and global leaders promote multilateral diplomacy, dialogue, and inclusive negotiations to de-escalate conflicts and strengthen Western unity.
  • China and APEC emphasize south–south cooperation, sovereignty, and coordinated multilateral pressure to reduce tensions and nuclear risks.
  • Regional conflicts in the Middle East and North Africa highlight the need for multilateral mediation, local initiatives, and practical measures for lasting peace.

Spain is advocating multilateral diplomacy as the primary route to de‑escalate global conflicts and to strengthen unity among Western allies. World leaders at summits and regional actors are emphasizing dialogue, restraint and inclusive negotiation formats while critics warn that exclusive arrangements like a 'Yalta' model would fail. Simultaneously, grassroots and UN‑led initiatives focus on prevention, rehabilitation and local conflict management as necessary complements to high‑level diplomacy. Regional flashpoints in the Middle East and North Africa, and China’s push for south‑south cooperation and respect for sovereignty, show competing diplomatic approaches that multilateral forums must reconcile.

European leaders and commentators argue that proposed 'Yalta'/'Jalte' formats would exclude key stakeholders and are unlikely to produce durable peace. They call for broader European participation and more inclusive negotiation frameworks to resolve crises such as the war in Ukraine.
