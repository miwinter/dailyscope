Starship catch milestones and ongoing tests

SpaceX Starship Catch 2026-11-15 • 20 articles in total

In brief
  • SpaceX successfully recovered Starship’s upper stage using launch‑tower arms after orbital flight, advancing steps toward full vehicle reusability.
  • A booster catch failed, highlighting technical challenges; SpaceX will analyze and improve recovery techniques.
  • Upcoming tests include attempting dual-stage catch, emphasizing an aggressive, iterative approach to reusable rocket development.

SpaceX’s Starship test series has reached a pivotal moment with multiple reports of the upper stage and vehicle being caught or recovered after orbital flight, marking a major step toward full reusability. Footage and accounts indicate the tower’s mechanical arms and controlled descents enabled recovery at the launch site, and observers say the achievement could lower turnaround times and launch costs for future Moon and Mars missions. Not all elements went perfectly: a booster catch attempt reportedly failed and will be subject to further analysis. SpaceX is continuing aggressive testing, including planned double‑catch trials, as it refines the technology.

Successful captures and landings

Multiple outlets report SpaceX recovered Starship’s upper stage and/or vehicle using the launch‑tower mechanical arms or landed it back at the pad after orbital flight. Observers frame these recoveries as a key milestone toward reusability that could cut costs and enable frequent crewed and cargo missions to the Moon, Mars and low Earth orbit.

Booster catch aborted

At least one report highlights a failed booster catch attempt, underscoring the technical risks and complexity of mid‑air recovery maneuvers. SpaceX is expected to review the anomaly to identify causes and plan subsequent flights.

Planned double‑catch and continued testing

SpaceX is preparing even bolder tests, including an announced plan to attempt catching both stages in a single flight, signaling an iterative test program that accepts failures as part of development. These plans reflect the company’s aggressive timeline to validate fully reusable operations.
