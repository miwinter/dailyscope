Great Power Rivalry

Great Power Rivalry 2026-11-24 • 19 articles in total

In brief
  • US, Russia, and China competition reshapes global security, increasing nuclear risks and regional conflicts beyond traditional theaters.
  • European political shifts and public division weaken alliance unity, complicating coordinated responses to great-power rivalry.
  • Global South and new regions face intensified great-power influence efforts, expanding strategic competition and raising deterrence challenges.

Competition among the United States, Russia, and China is reshaping security dynamics across multiple theaters, from the Indo‑Pacific to the Mediterranean and Africa. Moscow’s escalating rhetoric and Moscow‑related actions risk nuclear brinkmanship and regional spillovers, while Beijing’s growing naval and technological capabilities challenge existing deterrence and arms‑control assumptions. European political realignments and wavering public faith in alliances complicate coordinated responses, and rising influence campaigns in the Global South extend the rivalry into new strategic spaces. The combined effect is a more fragmented, multipolar contest with heightened risks of local conflicts cascading into broader strategic confrontations.

Middle Eastern and Global South diplomacy, from the UAE’s outreach in the Comoros to France’s operations in the Mozambique Channel, shows great powers contesting influence far beyond traditional battlegrounds.
