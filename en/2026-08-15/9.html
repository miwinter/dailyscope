France rulings reshape youth online rules and platform obligations

France Digital Rights Ruling 2026-08-15 • 5 articles in total

In brief
  • France's Constitutional Council invalidated the social media ban for under-15s, citing minors' freedom and legal limits on age restrictions.
  • President Macron ordered a law review to enhance online child protections despite the Council's decision.
  • French courts address platform duties, with Meta disputing an order to pay publishers, highlighting tensions over digital responsibilities.

France's Constitutional Council struck down a law banning social media use for under-15s, prompting immediate debate over children's online safety and minors' freedom of expression. President Emmanuel Macron has ordered a review of the contested law, signaling continued executive interest in tightening protections despite the ruling. Advocates for stricter safeguards say the decision weakens protections for children, while civil liberties defenders and some commentators argue that age-based bans raise legal and practical concerns. Separately, French courts are also handling disputes over platform responsibilities, with Meta contesting an injunction to remunerate the press for news content. Together, these cases underscore a broader policy struggle in France over how to balance child protection, free expression, and platform obligations in the digital age.
