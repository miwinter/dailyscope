Russia–Ukraine attacks: strikes, drone raids and infrastructure fallout

Russia–Ukraine Attacks 2026-11-07 • 21 articles in total

In brief
  • Russian strikes hit Ukrainian cities and energy infrastructure, causing civilian deaths, damage, and widespread power and water outages.
  • Ukraine escalates cross-border drone attacks targeting Russian military and energy sites, including airspace over Moscow, expanding conflict zones.
  • Heightened fighting in eastern Ukraine and Black Sea drone attacks impact regional security amid political tensions delaying Ukrainian emergency responses.

Escalating Russian strikes continue to hit Ukrainian cities and energy infrastructure, causing dozens of civilian deaths, damage to schools and kindergartens, and nation‑wide power and water disruptions. Ukraine has intensified cross‑border operations and drone campaigns targeting Russian refineries, military systems and even airspace over Moscow, signalling an expansion of the battlefield. Frontline fighting grows volatile in Donetsk and other hotspots while visible political strains in Kyiv complicate emergency response and recovery. A separate maritime drone strike in the Black Sea and international commentary on heavy Russian casualties underline how the conflict now affects regional security, logistics and strategic narratives.

Russian strikes on civilians and critical infrastructure

Missile and bombing campaigns have repeatedly targeted Ukrainian population centers and energy systems, producing high civilian casualties and widespread damage to power plants, schools and housing. The attacks have caused emergency outages, water supply disruptions and urgent repair efforts, amplifying the humanitarian toll and straining local authorities.

Ukrainian counterstrikes and drone campaigns inside Russia

Ukrainian forces have conducted strikes and drone operations across the border, hitting oil refineries, missile systems and other military targets in multiple Russian regions and showing an ability to project force into Russian territory. The use of drones over Moscow and attacks on energy infrastructure are raising concerns about escalation and the vulnerability of strategic assets.

Frontline shifts and urban escalation

Fighting intensifies in Donetsk and other eastern sectors, with local observers noting changing dynamics on the front while some areas near Kharkiv report little immediate impact. Reports of unprecedented scenes in Kyiv and strong leadership rhetoric reflect heightened urban tensions and the risk of further escalation.

Black Sea maritime security incident

A drone strike near the Black Sea forced Bulgarian rescue ships to halt a search for missing seafarers, underscoring growing risks to commercial and rescue traffic in regional waters. The incident highlights how hostilities are extending beyond land into maritime routes and civilian shipping safety.

Governance strain and supply crisis in Kyiv

Damage to energy infrastructure has provoked large‑scale supply failures in Kyiv and exposed tensions between President Zelensky and the city mayor, complicating coordination of relief and repairs. Political disputes over responsibility risk undermining public confidence and slowing recovery efforts during a critical period.

International analysis and casualty assessments

International voices and analyses place the fighting in a broader strategic frame, citing heavy Russian losses and Kremlin narratives about NATO as drivers of aggression. These perspectives shape diplomatic discussions on NATO unity, peace prospects and the wider security implications of the conflict.
