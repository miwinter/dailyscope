High‑stakes summit diplomacy: NATO, Trump’s mediation, and regional alignments

World Leaders Dialogue 2026-07-06 • 138 articles in total

In brief
  • US diplomacy under President Trump relies on direct leader calls and summit meetings to mediate conflicts including Ukraine and Syria.
  • NATO summit exposes European divisions over defense spending, Iran policy, and Turkey, prompting efforts to maintain alliance unity.
  • China and Russia strengthen military and economic ties while Indo-Pacific, Middle Eastern, and regional states pursue varied security, diplomatic, and development cooperation amid global tensions.

Leaders and foreign ministers converged around a series of high‑profile meetings and calls ahead of a NATO summit, with U.S. President Donald Trump actively re‑engaging—holding multiple calls with Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelenskiy and arranging side‑meetings to push for mediation on Ukraine and discussions with Syria. European capitals warned of alliance strains over burden‑sharing, Iran policy and Turkey’s balancing act, while NATO hosts scrambled to preserve cohesion and prepare major procurement and security pledges. At the same time China and Russia continued to deepen military cooperation and showcase joint exercises, even as Beijing advanced diplomatic outreach and the Belt and Road narrative. Regional diplomacy remained busy: France and other Western states signalled renewed engagement with Syria, Gulf and Iran actors negotiated maritime and security arrangements, and Indo‑Pacific partners sharpened ties on defence, technology and green transition. Smaller states and regional organizations also pursued bilateral goodwill and pragmatic cooperation across trade, migration and development, reflecting a multilayered diplomatic environment.
