Shifts and strains in US diplomacy

US Diplomacy Watch 2026-09-17 • 72 articles in total

In brief
  • US foreign policy combines security measures, arms transfers, and diplomacy, causing international friction and domestic debate.
  • Operations in Latin America and Middle East focus on counter-narcotics, visa restrictions, and political tensions.
  • US pursues global engagement amid concerns about credibility, alliance strains, and democratic erosion.

Recent coverage shows a US foreign-policy agenda focused on security operations, arms transfers, and diplomatic outreach while provoking friction with partners and critics. Washington's counter-narcotics operations and aid reallocation have reshaped relations across Latin America even as visa bans and denials for Palestinian leaders have aggravated Middle East diplomacy. Congressional resistance and reporting about arms shipments and ammunition strains highlight contested domestic debates over support to Israel and messaging on the Iran war, while blunt rhetoric from the White House has heightened transatlantic tensions. At the same time, the United States is pursuing targeted engagement—from talks with the Houthis and Belarus to deeper Pacific ties and AI dialogue with China—against a backdrop of global concerns about US credibility, democratic erosion, and economic confidence.
