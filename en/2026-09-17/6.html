Fed’s 25bp Hike Signals Further Tightening Amid Global and Political Ripples

US Fed Rate Hike 2026-09-17 • 25 articles in total

In brief
  • The U.S. Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate by 25 basis points to about 3.75–4.00% to combat persistent inflation.
  • Markets and analysts expect more rate increases, raising borrowing costs and causing global economic concerns.
  • Political tensions rose as the White House and President Trump criticized the hike while some Fed officials defended it as responsible policy.

The U.S. Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate by 25 basis points—its first increase since 2023—bringing the target range to roughly 3.75–4.00% as policymakers seek to curb persistent inflation. Officials signalled that additional tightening could follow, prompting markets to reprice bond yields, commodities and borrowing-cost expectations. Analysts warned of higher mortgage and loan costs and potential spillovers to global markets, while other commentators argued that standing pat could be riskier amid sticky inflation. The decision also amplified political tensions, with the White House and President Trump criticizing the hike even as some Fed officials defended the move as responsible policy.
