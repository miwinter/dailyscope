Bilateral deals reshape trade, security and regional connectivity

Region: Bilateral Deals 2026-09-17 • 106 articles in total

In brief
  • Bilateral agreements cover trade, infrastructure, defence, development, and strategic partnerships globally.
  • The EU seeks deeper ties with Canada via associate status amid mixed international reactions.
  • Countries use diplomacy and projects to boost connectivity, investment, security, and regional integration.

Recent bilateral agreements and initiatives span a wide set of priorities: trade and market access, strategic infrastructure and investment, defence cooperation, and targeted development assistance. The European Commission’s push to deepen ties with Canada through an unprecedented ‘associate member’ proposal has generated broad international reaction and dominates many European discussions. China’s outward investment and connectivity projects — from canals and rail links to resource and urban projects — continue to reshape regional trade routes and attract scrutiny over governance. Elsewhere, regional normalization and diplomatic upgrades (notably Israel–Morocco and related moves), large arms and security arrangements, and practical connectivity talks (air links, ports and border concessions) underscore that bilateral deals are being used both to anchor strategic partnerships and to accelerate economic ties.
