Global dialogue fractures and convergences: security, multilateralism and bilateral outreach

World Leaders Dialogue 2026-09-17 • 104 articles in total

In brief
  • The EU advances an autonomous security and strategic agenda with new partnerships beyond the US.
  • BRICS and Global South push multilateralism for broader global influence while Russia mixes openness to talks with rejecting Western security pacts.
  • Regional diplomacy is active across the Middle East, Africa, and Latin America addressing conflicts, sovereignty, and influence amid challenges like AI rivalry, climate change, and UN reform.

Leaders and institutions are engaging on multiple fronts to manage security, trade and global governance, with the EU pushing a more autonomous strategic course while BRICS and emerging powers emphasize multilateral alternatives. Russia’s mixed signals—ranging from openness to talks to rejecting security pacts—underscore ongoing geopolitical friction, paralleled by intensifying defence cooperation among Western allies. Regional diplomacy is highly active: Middle Eastern mediation, African state-to-state outreach, Latin American election- and influence-related exchanges, and numerous state visits and credential exchanges demonstrate continued reliance on bilateral channels. Cross-cutting issues such as AI rivalry, climate impacts (extreme heat, melting glaciers, Arctic strategy) and UN reform debates are shaping leaders’ agendas and complicating diplomatic alignments.
