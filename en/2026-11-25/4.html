Great Power Rivalry: Shifting Frontiers

Great Power Rivalry 2026-11-25 • 20 articles in total

In brief
  • Russia's military buildup near NATO and activities in Africa test alliances and risk wider conflicts, prompting increased NATO readiness and regional concern.
  • Western military aid strengthens Ukraine but faces political fatigue, complicating sustained support and strategy against Russian advances.
  • China expands influence in the Indo-Pacific through mixed partnerships, while US perceived weaknesses encourage rival assertiveness and proxy conflicts escalate.

Great power competition is reshaping security across Europe, Africa and the Indo-Pacific as the US, Russia and China project military, diplomatic and informational power. Russia’s military buildups near NATO borders and its activities in Africa and the Middle East test alliance cohesion and risk regional spillover, while Western military aid to Ukraine is both strengthening Kyiv and strained by domestic political fatigue. Perceptions of US weakness under changing leadership encourage strategic opportunism by rivals, and China’s mix of partnerships and pressure tactics is reshaping influence in the Pacific and beyond. Local conflicts are increasingly arenas for strategic signaling and proxy competition, complicating efforts to stabilize regional orders.
