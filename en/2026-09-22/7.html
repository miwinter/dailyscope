Evidence and evaluation shaping policy choices

Evidence-Based Policy 2026-09-22 • 8 articles in total

In brief
  • Governments base policy decisions on demographic data and evidence from health, education, and infrastructure to target resources effectively.
  • Health and safety issues prompt prevention and investigation efforts while education reforms focus on evidence-backed measures like cellphone bans.
  • Funding and program planning prioritize proven impact, monitored outcomes, and caution when risks arise to ensure accountability and safety.

Across these reports, governments and institutions are relying on data, consultation and evaluation to shape policy priorities from health to education and infrastructure. Demographic and usage statistics—such as Japan’s record elderly share and New Zealand’s rail patronage—are driving planning and funding decisions. Public health and safety concerns, including rising childhood type 2 diabetes in Malaysia and scrutiny of Indonesia’s school meals, are prompting calls for prevention, screening and investigations. Education policy in Mexico is moving toward a nationwide cellphone ban backed by teacher consultation to improve learning, while funding in other countries is being steered toward programmes demonstrably proven to work and monitored for impact. Overall, the pieces highlight a tilt toward evidence-based targeting of resources, balanced by implementation challenges and scrutiny when risks emerge.

Countries covering this topic

Japan demographic data drives policy

Official demographic statistics showing a historic high share of elderly citizens are presented as a key driver of social security and labor market policy adjustments. The perspective stresses planning for long-term fiscal and service implications of an aging population.

Public-health prevention in Malaysia

Health authorities frame rising childhood type 2 diabetes as a growing non-communicable disease challenge that requires prevention, early screening and education. The view prioritizes public-health interventions and long-term monitoring to curb future burdens.

  • Type 2 Diabetes a growing worry among children in Malaysia

Mexico cellphone ban — implementation focus (source: COL)

This account emphasizes the practicalities of enforcing a nationwide school cellphone ban, listing exceptions and discussing potential impacts on students, teachers and parents. The perspective highlights the expected learning benefits alongside oversight and operational challenges.

  • No cell phones in Mexican schools from November 3: these will be the exceptions

Mexico cellphone ban — consultation-backed policy (source: PER)

Presented as the outcome of wide consultation with teachers, this report frames the ban (including recess) as evidence-informed and supported by frontline educators to reduce distractions and improve classroom conditions. The focus is on scope, enforcement and the legitimacy lent by majority support among consulted teachers.

  • Mexico to ban cellphone use in schools: measure will extend to recess

Funding what works — evaluation push

The perspective calls for allocating public funds only to programmes proven effective through rigorous evaluation, arguing evaluations should decide which initiatives to scale up or discontinue. It emphasizes accountability and efficiency in development and domestic spending.

  • Leigh urges governments to fund only the programmes evidence shows work

Data-backed investment in services and infrastructure

These reports use outcome data to justify continued or expanded investment: rising rail patronage is presented as evidence to support transport planning, while new funding for frontline services is framed as a monitored intervention to prevent violence against women. The common view is that measurable results should guide planning and resource allocation.

  • New Zealand: City Rail Link Train Patronage A Record-Breaker
  • Great Britain: Frontline services to receive funding to prevent violence against women and girls

Scrutiny of social programme safety

Following a reported adverse health incident, commentators urge pausing Indonesia’s free meals programme pending investigation, reflecting a precautionary stance that weighs programme benefits against potential risks. The view underscores the need for safety monitoring and evidence before scaling or continuing interventions.

  • Calls to halt Indonesia free meals program as student loses memory
