Bilateral Deals: Trade, Security and Regional Diplomacy Accelerate

Region: Bilateral Deals 2026-08-03 • 94 articles in total

In brief
  • Major powers and smaller states deepen trade, infrastructure, and security ties through deals, visits, and agreements across multiple regions.
  • China promotes regional influence via technology, transport, and business partnerships without military involvement.
  • Diplomatic and security mechanisms advance to manage strategic areas, enhance cooperation, and address migration and defense challenges.

Recent bilateral activity highlights a wide-ranging push on trade, infrastructure and security across regions, with major powers and smaller states alike deepening ties through commercial deals, MOUs and high-level visits. China’s outreach spans technology, transport and Pacific partnerships, while Iran and Oman pursue a diplomatic route to manage Strait of Hormuz security. Morocco’s diplomatic gains—with U.S. backing on Western Sahara and expanding trade links—sit alongside numerous African and Pacific cooperation initiatives focused on investment, connectivity and development. Migration, visa regimes and consular arrangements remain salient, prompting renegotiations and operational changes in several states. Defense and security agreements, including arms, policing and counter-threat commitments, also figure prominently amid broader economic diplomacy.
