Trump announces talks as conditional pause meets Tehran denials

US–Iran Diplomacy 2026-08-03 • 110 articles in total

In brief
  • President Trump announced resumed talks with Iran and paused strikes conditionally on quick agreements to avoid escalation.
  • Iran denied agreeing to pause strikes and showed internal divisions over negotiations between hardliners and reformists.
  • Regional actors mediated for de-escalation while markets fell on hopes of diplomacy amid doubts about any lasting deal.

President Donald Trump repeatedly announced the resumption of direct talks with Iran and said he was pausing or cancelling planned strikes conditionally on reaching a quick agreement, presenting diplomacy as a way to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and avoid immediate escalation. Tehran pushed back against several of Mr. Trump’s claims, denying requests to pause strikes or that any agreement had been reached, while Iranian leaders remained internally divided between hardliners and reformists over negotiating. Regional actors — including Gulf states, Oman and Pakistan — emerged as active mediators, urging restraint and pressing both Washington and Tehran toward a diplomatic window even as some partners urged a firmer line. Markets and analysts reacted strongly: oil prices fell on hopes of de‑escalation, while commentators warned that gaps between rhetoric and action and broader strategic doubts make any durable deal uncertain and fragile.
