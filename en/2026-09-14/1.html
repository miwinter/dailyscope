Summits, summitry and crisis management: Leaders juggling multipolar ambitions and regional security

World Leaders Dialogue 2026-09-14 • 138 articles in total

In brief
  • World leaders use summits and bilateral visits to manage security, advance economic ties, and push institutional reforms.
  • Multilateral forums like BRICS focus on a multipolar agenda and urge restraint on Middle East escalation.
  • Diplomacy emphasizes crisis management, symbolic gestures, and coordination amid unresolved disputes and political constraints.

This set of articles shows world leaders using summits and bilateral visits to manage security risks, advance economic ties and press institutional reforms. BRICS and other multilateral forums dominated coverage, with members pushing a multipolar agenda while urging restraint on Middle East escalation and exploring expanded cooperation. High-level bilateral diplomacy ranged from defense cooperation and counter‑terrorism talks to state visits and economic outreach, even as domestic politics and regional crises — from Nigeria–South Africa xenophobic tensions to the Ukraine war — constrained immediate breakthroughs. Debates over UN reform, a new UN world map and proposals to reshape global governance illustrated competing visions of representation and influence. Across regions the pattern is familiar: strong rhetoric, targeted cooperation and continued fragility where underlying disputes remain unresolved.
