Guinea-Bissau referendum approves stronger presidency amid low turnout and legitimacy concerns

Guinea-Bissau Constitutional Referendum 2026-09-03 • 6 articles in total

In brief
  • Guinea-Bissau voters approved a new constitution with about 70% 'Yes' amid 37% turnout under military rule.
  • The revised constitution shifts power to the president, expanding control over government and parliament dissolution.
  • Opposition questions legitimacy and calls for reforms, while authorities defend the referendum and await Supreme Court confirmation.

Voters in Guinea-Bissau approved a new constitution in a referendum that election authorities say delivered about 70% 'Yes' amid a reported turnout near 37%. The revised text would shift the country toward a presidential system, expanding the president’s powers over the prime minister, government composition and the ability to dissolve parliament. The vote took place under a military junta that seized power last year, prompting opposition parties and civil society to denounce restrictions, call for a boycott and question the process’s legitimacy. Authorities and the National Election Commission defended the credibility of the poll while observers and analysts urged dialogue and further governance reforms; final results were to be confirmed by the Supreme Court.
