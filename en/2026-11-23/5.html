Great Power Rivalry: shaping a multipolar order

Great Power Rivalry 2026-11-23 • 20 articles in total

In brief
  • The U.S., Russia, and China vie to reshape global order via military, diplomacy, and ideology, impacting regions beyond Europe including Africa and the Indo-Pacific.
  • Russia escalates militarily in and beyond Ukraine, increasing regional conflicts and NATO's strategic challenges, raising risks of wider crisis and nuclear accidents.
  • China promotes a multipolar world through diplomacy and summits, challenging U.S. dominance, while all powers contest influence through regional disputes and emerging space weaponry.

The U.S., Russia and China are competing to redefine global order through military, diplomatic and ideological means, with effects that spill beyond Europe into Africa, the Middle East and the Indo-Pacific. China projects a Yalta-like vision of multipolarity and diplomacy, while Russia leverages kinetic escalation and asymmetric operations to challenge Western influence. Washington and NATO face alliance strains, deterrence dilemmas and novel threats such as space weaponization, driving debates about preparedness and strategy. Regional contests—from the Mozambique Channel to Gulf disputes—show how great power rivalry amplifies local conflicts and complicates security cooperation.
