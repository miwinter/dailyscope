France's under-15 social media ban blocked, review ordered

France Online Safety Law 2026-08-16 • 5 articles in total

In brief
  • France's highest courts rejected bans on social media for under-15s as disproportionate and violating free expression.
  • The courts emphasize constitutional limits on government control over digital platforms.
  • President Macron assigned Minister Lecornu to revise the law to protect children online while respecting constitutional rights.

France's highest judicial bodies have rejected or overturned proposed bans on social media use for under-15s, describing such measures as disproportionate and an excessive interference with freedom of expression. Multiple international reports note that the Constitutional Council and other courts blocked President Macron's plan to bar younger teens from social networks, underscoring constitutional limits on state intervention online. At the same time, President Macron has tasked Minister Sébastien Lecornu with reviewing the law, signaling the executive still seeks policy solutions to protect children online. The debate now centers on balancing child safety, enforceability and digital rights, with implications for families, tech platforms and potential legislative adjustments.
