Lenín Moreno convicted in high‑profile bribery case

Ecuador Corruption Trial 2026-08-29 • 13 articles in total

In brief
  • Lenín Moreno was convicted and sentenced to five years in prison for accepting bribes related to the Coca Codo Sinclair hydroelectric project.
  • The case involves alleged bribes from a Chinese company and highlights Ecuador's anti-corruption efforts.
  • Moreno's conviction reflects broader Latin American trends of prosecuting senior officials and influences regional political dynamics.

Former Ecuadorian president Lenín Moreno has been found guilty of accepting bribes tied to public contracts and financing for the Coca Codo Sinclair hydroelectric project, and multiple outlets report a five‑year prison sentence. Some reports note additional penalties such as bans from public office and charges against dozens of associates, portraying the ruling as part of a wider anti‑corruption push. Several articles emphasize alleged payments from a Chinese company and trace the scheme to preferential treatment for contractors and financing arrangements. The conviction has prompted appeals and polarized reactions, and regional commentary frames the case within broader Latin American efforts to hold senior officials to account.
