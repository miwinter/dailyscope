Eisenkot tasked to form Israeli government

Israel Coalition Talks 2026-11-08 • 50 articles in total

In brief
  • President Isaac Herzog appointed former army chief Gadi Eisenkot to form a new Israeli government after a deadlocked election.
  • Eisenkot faces complex coalition talks in a divided Knesset, with Arab party support crucial but controversial, risking government instability.
  • This mandate marks a setback for Benjamin Netanyahu, whose warnings against Arab-inclusive coalitions highlight deep political divisions and election risks.

After a deadlocked election, President Isaac Herzog has tasked former army chief Gadi Eisenkot with forming a new government, giving him the usual 28-day mandate (extendable) to assemble a coalition. Reports emphasise the start of intensive negotiations in a fragmented Knesset where Arab party endorsements and cross-bloc deals could be decisive. Many outlets frame the move as a political setback for Benjamin Netanyahu, who has warned that a government built with Arab party support would threaten the state’s cohesion. Analysts warn that the coalition math, vetoes and competing policy demands make any resulting government fragile and leave open the possibility of another election.
