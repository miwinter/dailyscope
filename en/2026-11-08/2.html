Miller named chief of staff; Dana White courted

US: Trump Staff Shakeup 2026-11-08 • 32 articles in total

In brief
  • President Trump replaced chief of staff Susie Wiles with hardline ally Stephen Miller, signaling a tougher policy shift, especially on immigration.
  • He sought unconventional figures like UFC boss Dana White for senior communications roles, causing surprise and mixed reactions.
  • These changes reflect a post-midterm effort to consolidate leadership, tighten messaging, and reshape Republican strategy amid resignations.

After a disappointing midterm showing, President Trump replaced chief of staff Susie Wiles with Stephen Miller, a hardline ally, signaling a consolidation of his inner circle and a likely shift toward tougher policies, especially on immigration. The personnel changes form part of a broader post-election shake-up that included resignations and the possibility of further departures. Trump also publicly courted unconventional figures for senior communications roles—most notably UFC boss Dana White—prompting surprise and mixed reactions domestically and abroad. Critics warn the moves risk eroding institutional norms and increasing polarization, while supporters view them as an effort to tighten discipline, messaging, and policy focus.

Consolidation of hardliners and unconventional hires

The appointment of Stephen Miller is read as a deliberate pivot toward loyalist, hardline governance likely to harden immigration and domestic policy stances. Concurrent efforts to recruit outsiders like Dana White for top communications roles underline an unconventional personnel strategy that has provoked controversy and media attention. Together these actions signal a post-midterm attempt to reconfigure leadership, messaging, and Republican strategy ahead.
