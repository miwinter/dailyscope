Regional bilateral deals: security pacts, restored ties and economic links

Region: Bilateral Deals 2026-09-23 • 81 articles in total

In brief
  • Governments signed diverse bilateral agreements covering defence, diplomacy, trade, development, and security, notably a multi-party Arctic security pact involving Greenland.
  • Diplomatic relations were restored or expanded to enhance cooperation in trade, aid, military partnerships, and technical development projects across sectors.
  • Concerns about migration, transparency, and accountability accompanied economic, security, and development initiatives, highlighting challenges in governance and public trust.

Over the period covered, governments concluded a broad set of bilateral agreements spanning defence, diplomacy, trade and development. The most prominent thread is a multi-party security accord centred on Greenland that drew attention from the United States, Denmark, Greenland and European partners, prompting relief as well as reservations locally. Several countries re-established or newly opened formal diplomatic ties while others deepened economic links through trade deals, credit lines and infrastructure projects. Parallel initiatives addressed security cooperation and military assistance beyond the Arctic, and a range of development and technical partnerships targeted water, forestry, health and transport upgrades. Migration, transparency and concerns about arms transfers or accountability also surfaced alongside these cooperation milestones.
