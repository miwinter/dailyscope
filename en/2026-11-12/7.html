Spain: General strike protests and arrests

Spain General Strike Protests 2026-11-12 • 25 articles in total

In brief
  • A nationwide general strike in Spain involved around four million workers protesting housing costs and cost-of-living increases.
  • Violent clashes occurred in Madrid and Barcelona, including looting and arson, leading to police use of tear gas and multiple arrests.
  • The strike highlighted deep social unrest and increased political pressure before an upcoming election, with unions demanding urgent housing and wage reforms.

A nationwide general strike in Spain, described as the first in 14 years, drew millions of workers and widespread protests focused on housing costs and the rising cost of living. While most demonstrations began peacefully, substantial disturbances erupted in Madrid and Barcelona, including looting, arson and clashes with police. Law enforcement detained dozens of people, used crowd-control measures such as tear gas, and reported multiple arrests across cities. The unrest underscored deep social discontent and posed political pressure ahead of an upcoming election, while unions pushed for urgent housing and wage reforms.

Clashes, arrests and street unrest

Multiple reports highlight violent confrontations in Madrid and Barcelona, with police detentions, tear gas and arrests as authorities tried to restore order. Incidents included looting, property damage and rapid escalation from protests to clashes, prompting significant law-enforcement responses across several cities.

Scale, causes and political context of the strike

Coverage emphasizes the strike’s massive scale—often put at around four million participants—and frames it as a response to a housing crisis, inflation and broader cost-of-living pressures. Unions organized the nationwide action as the first general strike in 14 years, disrupting transport and services and adding political pressure on the government ahead of an early election.
