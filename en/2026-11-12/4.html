US Midterms: turnout, counting and a historic Mississippi upset

US Midterm Elections Coverage 2026-11-12 • 28 articles in total

In brief
  • Democrats gained significant seats in the 2026 midterms, highlighted by Scott Colom's historic win as Mississippi's first Black senator in over a century.
  • Delayed counting of mail-in and provisional ballots delayed results and raised scrutiny, amidst fraud claims from former President Trump.
  • The elections marked a rejection of Trump-aligned strategies, impacting Republican leadership and raising concerns about election trust and future party dynamics.

Democrats made significant gains in the 2026 U.S. midterms, capped by a historic Democratic victory in Mississippi where Scott Colom became the state’s first Black senator in over a century. Slow counting of mail-in and provisional ballots delayed final results in several key races, fueling public scrutiny and media attention. Claims of fraud from former President Trump and a shaken MAGA ecosystem followed the losses, even as analysts emphasise the implications for party strategy and governance. The combination of turnout dynamics, counting practices, and campaign outcomes will shape policy debates and electoral tactics going forward.
