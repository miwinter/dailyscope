US–Russia diplomacy: envoys, security talks and a sudden death

US–Russia Diplomacy 2026-11-12 • 30 articles in total

In brief
  • US envoys Kushner and Witkoff engage in shuttle diplomacy between Abu Dhabi, Moscow, and Dublin to advance peace talks and prisoner exchanges with limited breakthroughs.
  • Washington urges Russia for European security proposals and broader peace talks participation, exploring trilateral engagement with China amidst careful messaging and uncertainty.
  • The sudden death of Russia’s US ambassador, Alexander Darchiev, and involvement of private business actors add complexity and unpredictability to diplomatic efforts.

A US‑led diplomatic push has seen envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff shuttle between Abu Dhabi, Moscow and Dublin after US‑mediated talks aimed at de‑escalation and prisoner exchanges, though no decisive breakthrough has yet been reported. Washington is pressing Moscow for broader participation in peace negotiations and for concrete security proposals for Europe, while exploring high‑level trilateral engagement with Russia and China. Russian officials publicly discussed the Abu Dhabi outcomes and private business actors accompanied the diplomatic traffic, highlighting the mixture of formal and informal channels. The sudden, unexplained death of Russia’s ambassador adds an element of uncertainty to fragile engagement between the two capitals.

Sudden death of Russia’s ambassador

Multiple outlets reported the sudden death of Alexander (Alexandre) Darchiev, Russia’s ambassador to the United States, at age 66, a development noted for its timing amid renewed Moscow‑Washington engagement. Officials have provided few details, and his unexpected death introduces further unpredictability to ongoing diplomatic efforts.
