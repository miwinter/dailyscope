Escalation in the Red Sea Threatens Shipping and Energy Markets

Red Sea Maritime Security 2026-07-23 • 26 articles in total

In brief
  • Houthis claim responsibility for attacks on Red Sea tankers, enforcing a blockade at Bab al-Mandeb and targeting ships they accuse of violations.
  • Regional governments support Saudi Arabia, confirm attacks, and call for protection of shipping lanes and de-escalation.
  • U.S.-Iran tensions and attacks raise fears of wider conflict, causing oil price rises and shipping route disruptions.

A wave of attacks and projectile strikes in the Red Sea—many claimed by Yemen’s Houthi movement—has damaged or set fire to commercial tankers and prompted vessel diversions. The Houthis have declared a blockade at Bab al-Mandeb and said strikes targeted ships they say violated that blockade, raising the risk to a key global chokepoint. Regional governments and Gulf allies have publicly backed Saudi Arabia and confirmed attacks, calling for security measures and de-escalation. Washington’s threats and strikes on Iran, and analysts’ warnings, have heightened fears of a broader confrontation, while oil prices and shipping routes have already reacted to the disruption. International authorities are warning of continued maritime risks and potential knock-on effects for global trade and energy markets.
