UN vote on Cuba embargo

Cuba Embargo Resolution 2026-10-29 • 18 articles in total

In brief
  • The UN General Assembly condemned the U.S. embargo on Cuba with 130 votes in favor, showing broad international opposition despite U.S. pressure.
  • Cuban officials emphasized the embargo's severe humanitarian and economic impact, defending sovereignty and calling for its immediate end.
  • Some countries abstained or opposed the resolution, reflecting shifting diplomatic pressures and divisions in the hemisphere.

The United Nations General Assembly again adopted a resolution condemning the U.S. embargo on Cuba, with 130 votes in favor, 17 against and 25 abstentions, showing broad international support but evidence of pressure on several states. Many countries and regional figures publicly urged an immediate, unconditional end to the blockade, while some governments abstained or opposed the measure, reflecting shifting diplomatic calculations. Cuba used the debate to highlight the humanitarian and economic harms of the blockade and to defend its sovereignty, and Cuban officials clashed with U.S. diplomats during the proceedings. The outcome underscores persistent global opposition to the embargo alongside ongoing U.S. efforts to sustain pressure and sway votes.

Cuban government stance

Cuban officials framed the UN debate as proof of international backing and emphasized the blockade's harmful humanitarian and economic effects, calling for its immediate end. Foreign Minister Bruno Rodríguez outlined procedural conditions for the debate and repeatedly defended Cuba's sovereignty, accusing U.S. policy of coercion.

International supporters and appeals

A wide range of states and regional actors publicly supported the resolution to end the embargo, urging unconditional relief and closer economic ties with Cuba. Statements and votes from countries such as Panama, Andorra and Vietnam, along with appeals from former CARICOM leaders, signaled broad multilateral pressure on Washington.

UN vote, results and regional dynamics

The annual vote passed 130–17 with 25 abstentions, but reporting noted that backing has eroded under pressure from the Trump administration, producing more abstentions and a few votes against. Analysis emphasized diplomatic splits in the hemisphere and the political calculation behind shifting voting patterns.

U.S. position, confrontations and allegations

U.S. diplomats defended the embargo and engaged in public confrontations with Cuban representatives at the UN, while advancing narratives and allegations intended to justify continued pressure. Commentators also highlighted U.S. efforts to frame the vote as a diplomatic contest and to claim tactical wins despite the majority opposing the blockade.
