Russia–Ukraine Attacks: infrastructure strikes and frontline pressure

Russia–Ukraine Attacks 2026-11-19 • 50 articles in total

In brief
  • Ukrainian strikes repeatedly damaged the Crimean Kerch bridge, halting Russian supply routes and causing significant symbolic and logistical challenges.
  • Long-range Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries reduced energy capacity, undermining Russia's military logistics and economic sustainability.
  • Heavy Donbas fighting and drone attacks increase civilian suffering and security risks, escalating tensions with cross-border incidents and intensified frontline battles.

Ukrainian and Russian forces continue a high-intensity campaign of attacks that increasingly target critical infrastructure as well as frontline positions. Repeated Ukrainian strikes on the Crimean/Kerch bridge have caused partial collapse and halted traffic, threatening Russian supply lines to Crimea and prompting repair vows and international concern. Simultaneously, long-range strikes and drone operations have damaged Russian oil refineries and energy hubs, degrading processing capacity and complicating logistics. On the front lines, Russian offensives around Donbas towns such as Kostjantynivka are wearing down civilians while drone-enabled tactics and cross-border incidents raise the risk of further escalation.

Crimean/Kerch bridge attacks and their fallout

A sustained Ukrainian campaign has repeatedly struck the Crimean (Kerch) bridge, producing partial collapses, halted vehicle and rail traffic and widespread disruption to the link between Russia and occupied Crimea. Coverage emphasizes both the strategic effects on Russian logistics and the symbolic blow to Moscow, alongside Russian promises to repair and international concern about escalation.

Strikes on Russian energy infrastructure

Ukrainian long-range strikes and specialized units have targeted Russian refineries and oil infrastructure, reportedly cutting processing at major plants and inflicting significant damage to energy capacity. Analysts frame these attacks as aimed at degrading Russia’s logistical and economic ability to sustain military operations.

Drone warfare and tactical evolution

Mass drone use is reshaping the battlefield: swarm attacks and remote strikes are penetrating defenses, prompting new tactics such as subterranean sheltering and sustained campaigns to bypass Russian air defenses. Reporting argues drones have achieved effects that sanctions and conventional strikes did not, altering operational calculus on both sides.

Frontline fighting, civilian toll and wider security incidents

Heavy fighting in Donbas, notably around Kostjantynivka and Donetsk, continues to exhaust local populations while Kyiv insists on stiff defence and international support. The conflict’s reach includes journalist deaths, cross-border strikes and urban incidents (including a Moscow car bombing investigation), underscoring the war’s humanitarian and security repercussions.
