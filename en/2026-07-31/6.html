Mass crossings strain Ceuta border between Spain and Morocco

Morocco–Spain Ceuta Migration 2026-07-31 • 47 articles in total

In brief
  • Thousands of migrants surged into Ceuta from Morocco, overwhelming local controls and causing dozens of drownings during chaotic crossings.
  • Spain deployed troops, closed crossings, and increased cooperation with Morocco to manage the crisis and repatriate migrants.
  • The event sparked EU-level political debates over border security, migration policy, and burden-sharing among member states.

A sudden, large-scale surge of migrants from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta has overwhelmed local controls, with thousands attempting to cross by swimming, on foot, or by breaching fences. Reports across outlets describe chaotic scenes, large crowds on the Moroccan side, and dozens of fatalities in the water as rescue teams and authorities struggle to respond. The Spanish government has deployed military forces and security personnel, closed crossings in some areas, and stepped up coordination with Moroccan authorities on repatriations. International and EU-level reactions — including calls for tightened controls and debates about Schengen implications — have added a diplomatic dimension to the unfolding humanitarian emergency.
