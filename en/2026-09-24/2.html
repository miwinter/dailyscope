Bilateral Deals: Security, Investment and Connectivity Drive New Partnerships

Region: Bilateral Deals 2026-09-24 • 96 articles in total

In brief
  • States deepen bilateral security ties and manage diplomatic tensions to enhance regional stability.
  • Countries pursue economic diplomacy, large investments, and connectivity projects to boost growth and integration.
  • Major powers use trade truces and partnerships amid geopolitical rivalry and sectoral cooperation across governance and infrastructure.

This set of articles shows a surge in bilateral deals across security, economic and connectivity domains as states pursue strategic partnerships and practical cooperation. Governments are deepening defence and security ties, negotiating base handovers, and conducting cross-border operations while also managing diplomatic tensions and sanctions. Economic diplomacy is prominent: large investment pledges, trade agreements, investor roadshows and tourism stakes are shaping many countries' external engagement. Infrastructure, transport and connectivity pacts—from air services and subsea cables to major China-backed projects—accompany sectoral cooperation in health, education and governance. Together these developments reflect a pragmatic, transaction-focused wave of diplomacy aimed at boosting security, growth and regional integration despite recurring disputes and geopolitical rivalry.
