Global Spotlight on Media Credibility and Threats to Press Freedom

Global Media Accountability 2026-07-24 • 7 articles in total

In brief
  • Global media face rising mistrust due to political influence, misinformation, and challenges in accountability.
  • Journalists endure criminalisation, digital harassment, and political pressure threatening press freedom worldwide.
  • AI technologies transform media production, raising new concerns about trust, ethics, and editorial control.

Recent reporting highlights a global debate over media credibility, from state-run broadcasters apologizing for past misinformation to viral clips whose authenticity remains unclear. International watchdogs and press groups warn of increasing criminalisation, digital harassment, and political pressure that undermine journalists’ safety and independence. In Europe, controversies around state media and political influence have prompted broader questions about public broadcaster accountability. Simultaneously, technological shifts—particularly AI-driven media projects—are reshaping content production and raising new concerns about trust and editorial standards.

European state media and political influence

Coverage from Europe emphasizes the tension between public broadcasters and political power: a Hungarian state channel's dramatic apology is framed as a wake-up call about editorial standards, while reporting on Serbia highlights journalists fleeing and criticism of political leaders. Together these pieces point to eroding trust in state-linked media and the need for stronger accountability mechanisms in European public broadcasting.

RSF alarms on repression and digital harassment in Africa

Reports invoking Reporters Without Borders document the criminalisation of journalism and the use of pro-regime digital militias to target reporters in Tunisia and Burkina Faso. These articles portray a systematic pattern of repression and call for international intervention to protect press freedom and the right to information.

  • Burkina Faso: RSF accuses pro-junta digital militia of targeting journalists
