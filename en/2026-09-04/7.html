VW reshapes workforce with 50k–100k job reductions

Germany: VW Job Cuts 2026-09-04 • 9 articles in total

In brief
  • Volkswagen plans to cut up to 100,000 jobs by the end of the decade to support electrification and software-focused vehicles.
  • The job reductions include an immediate 50,000 role cut to reduce costs amid tariffs, overcapacity, and Chinese competition.
  • The company aims for a phased, negotiated approach with retraining, keeping plants open initially, and focusing on future competitiveness.

Volkswagen has launched a major restructuring to cut tens of thousands of jobs as it accelerates electrification and shifts toward software-driven vehicles. Some reports describe an immediate, board-approved plan to eliminate about 50,000 roles to slash costs and shore up competitiveness, while others present a broader, phased target of roughly 100,000 reductions by the end of the decade. The measures are framed as necessary to respond to tariffs, overcapacity and rising competition from Chinese rivals, and will affect workers, suppliers and regional communities across Europe and beyond. Several accounts note that factories may stay open for now and that retraining and phased implementation are part of the company’s approach, but timing and local impacts remain uncertain.

Long-term 100,000-job plan

These sources present VW’s announcement as a long-term, group-wide strategy to cut around 100,000 positions by the end of the decade, emphasizing a phased approach tied to electrification and a move toward software-focused vehicles. They highlight agreements with management and unions and frame the reductions as workforce repositioning to secure the company’s future competitiveness.
