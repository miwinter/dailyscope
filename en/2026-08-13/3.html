Trump presidency: secrecy, security, cyber push and legal battles

US: Trump Presidency 2026-08-13 • 78 articles in total

In brief
  • International media focus on Trump's secretive travel measures amid Iranian threats, raising concerns about security and intelligence-sharing.
  • Reports highlight U.S. regional posture, cyber authority expansions, legal challenges to Trump's businesses, and critiques of press freedom and political transparency.
  • Coverage includes U.S. diplomatic moves, domestic political strategies, and controversial health and science policy changes under Trump's administration.

International reporting around Donald Trump this cycle is dominated by revelations and debate over a covert aircraft switch and other secretive travel measures after a perceived Iranian threat, prompting questions about presidential security, intelligence-sharing and press access. Allied and regional outlets also amplify unease about U.S. posture toward Iran — from claims of control over the Strait of Hormuz to concerns about interceptor shortfalls and the credibility of threat warnings. Parallel policy moves draw attention: the administration’s widening use of cyber authorities (including private-sector partnerships) and contentious domestic changes to vaccination guidance and cancellation of scientific reports. At the same time, Trump faces a global wave of legal scrutiny over monetized platform access and business projects, while media and commentators across countries critique transparency, press safety and the political implications of his rhetoric.
