Global student activism challenging power

Global Student Activism 2026-10-27 • 5 articles in total

In brief
  • Student movements worldwide challenge entrenched power structures in media, government, and institutions.
  • Reports highlight harsh police responses to peaceful protests demanding scholarships and fair governance.
  • Organized student protests can weaken autocratic regimes and offer lessons in resilience and strategy.

Student movements in diverse regions are confronting entrenched power structures, from media influence in the Balkans to government authority in Albania and institutional inequality in Mauritania. Croatian outlets frame protests as a cultural and media backlash—targeting sensationalist programming and urging non-violence around press sites. Coverage from West Africa emphasizes heavy-handed police responses to peaceful demands over scholarships and housing, highlighting state repression and governance failures. Analyses of the Albanian case underline how organized student resistance can unseat or weaken autocratic practices, offering lessons on tactics, resilience, and the risks faced by young activists.

Croatian view: media-targeted, restrained protest

Croatian reporting frames student action as a response to damaging media culture and spectacle, portraying a clash between youth and sensationalist outlets. Coverage emphasizes calls from student leaders for non-violence and looks to Croatia as a model for organizing and conduct during demonstrations.

Senegalese view: policing and inequality in Mauritania

This perspective highlights a punitive security response to peaceful campus protests over scholarship and housing disparities, stressing injuries and heavy-handed policing. The coverage criticizes university governance and state repression that inflames controversy and undermines students' demands for equal access.

Mauritian view: mass street protests against Rama

Reporting focuses on large-scale student mobilization in Albania as a direct political challenge to Prime Minister Edi Rama, signaling broad youth discontent with education and governance. Accounts note significant police presence and uncertainty over whether the demonstrations will translate into policy change or political shifts.

  • Thousands of students protest Edi Rama in the streets
