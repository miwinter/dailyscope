Shifting security dynamics in the Pacific

Pacific Security Diplomacy 2026-08-18 • 9 articles in total

In brief
  • Pacific security features local cooperation alongside rising great-power competition and diplomatic tensions.
  • Taiwan enhances defense and communication resilience amid cross-strait tensions.
  • China's security activities challenge regional unity, prompting mixed responses from island states.

Coverage of Pacific security highlights competing trends: island-led cooperation and capacity-building coexist with intensifying great-power competition. Taiwan is strengthening deterrence and communications resilience as cross-strait tensions rise, while U.S. missile shortfalls and the redeployment of major naval assets raise questions about regional deterrence and readiness. China’s expanding security engagement and recent launches are testing regional unity and prompting both interest and caution among island states. Pacific voices are increasingly critical of Western militarisation and alliance approaches, demanding greater respect for sovereignty even as territorial disputes (such as over the Kurils) add further diplomatic strain.

This strand highlights anxiety over U.S. force posture — missile shortages and carrier redeployments are said to create shortfalls that could weaken deterrence in the Western Pacific. Analysts and local leaders call for clearer planning, funding, and transparency about defensive commitments.

This viewpoint criticizes Western policy for double standards and the tendency to prioritise alliance-based militarisation over regional autonomy. It calls for a reassessment of engagement to better respect Pacific voices and sovereignty in security arrangements.
