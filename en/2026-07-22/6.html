France bans social media for under-15s

France Minors Social Media Ban 2026-07-22 • 30 articles in total

In brief
  • France is the first EU country to ban social media access for users under 15 to protect children from online harms.
  • The law requires platforms to verify users' ages and includes youth-focused measures starting in September.
  • Critics question enforceability, privacy impacts, and effects on free expression amid awaited implementation details.

France's parliament has approved a law banning social media access for users under 15, positioning the country as the first in the European Union to adopt such a measure. The government frames the move as a child-protection initiative aimed at reducing exposure to online harms and data collection, and President Macron pushed for rapid enactment before the school term. The law delegates age-verification responsibilities to platforms and is part of a broader package of youth-focused measures, with some provisions slated to take effect in September. Journalists and commentators note the potential for the measure to influence EU digital regulation, while critics and privacy advocates question enforceability, privacy trade-offs and impacts on freedom of expression. Industry groups, lawmakers and civil society are awaiting implementation details and how platforms, families and schools will adapt.
