France Approves Assisted‑Dying Law with Strict Safeguards

France Assisted Dying Policy 2026-07-17 • 9 articles in total

In brief
  • France's National Assembly approved a law granting assisted dying rights for adults with terminal or incurable illnesses, with strict eligibility and safeguards.
  • The reform emphasizes patient autonomy and dignity, reflecting a major shift in French and broader European end-of-life policies.
  • The law's detailed framework includes procedural rules and protections, following years of national debate and ethical opposition.

France's National Assembly has approved legislation creating a statutory right to assisted dying for adults with terminal or incurable illnesses. The law lays out strict eligibility criteria, procedural safeguards and steps for implementation, following years of contentious debate. Supporters present the reform as a compassionate expansion of patient autonomy and dignity, while opponents warn of ethical risks and slippery‑slope concerns rooted in moral and religious objections. The vote represents a major shift in French end‑of‑life policy and is likely to influence broader European discussions on euthanasia and assisted suicide.
