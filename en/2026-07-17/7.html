US Tightens Visa Durations for Students and Journalists

US Student Visa Policy Change 2026-07-17 • 15 articles in total

In brief
  • The Trump administration set new limits on stays for foreign students and journalists to enhance security and visa integrity.
  • Critics warn the limits will harm academic exchanges, research collaboration, and foreign media reporting.
  • The rules specify maximum stays of four years for students and shorter periods for journalists, with some variations reported.

The Trump administration has moved to shorten how long foreign students, exchange visitors and journalists may stay in the United States, with proposals and final rules that set student stays at up to four years and limit journalists to shorter terms (commonly cited as 240 days, with some reporting of a 90-day cap for specific groups). Officials frame the changes as steps to strengthen security, oversight and the integrity of visa processes, and some items report the Department of Homeland Security issuing a final rule. Critics — including universities, researchers and press freedom advocates — warn the limits will harm academic exchanges, international research collaboration and foreign reporting from the U.S. The policy prompted extensive public comment and debate about implementation, diplomatic fallout and effects on enrollment and media access. Reporting across countries emphasizes either the administration’s security rationale, the specific new durations and procedures, or the potential adverse impacts on education and journalism.
