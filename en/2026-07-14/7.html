EU moves to tighten age-based access and verification on social media

EU Age Verification Social Media 2026-07-14 • 10 articles in total

In brief
  • EU bodies propose age limits and verification to protect minors on social media, focusing on banning unsupervised access under 13.
  • Debate exists between strict bans and gradual supervised access with parental controls to balance protection and participation.
  • Challenges include enforcement, technical feasibility, data limits, platform compliance, and child health and privacy concerns.

EU institutions and member states are advancing proposals to impose age-based limits and verification requirements on social media to better protect minors online. Proposals range from strict minimum-age rules—often focused on banning unsupervised access for under-13s—to phased, progressive access with parental controls and benchmarks. A recurring concern across reports is enforcement: mandatory age verification, limits on data collection and content, and the compliance burden on platforms. Child-health and privacy advocates frame the measures as necessary to reduce developmental harms and strengthen privacy, while debates persist over exact thresholds, parental roles and technical feasibility.
