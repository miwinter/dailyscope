Parliamentary move to remove Hungary's president tests constitutional order

Hungary Impeachment Proceedings 2026-07-14 • 13 articles in total

In brief
  • Hungary's parliament approved constitutional changes enabling the removal of the president and altering the Constitutional Court's role.
  • The amendments raise concerns about weakening checks and balances and reflect a political power struggle to limit Viktor Orbán's influence.
  • The episode tests Hungary's democratic institutions and has implications for EU relations and regional politics.

Hungary's parliament has approved constitutional amendments and laws that would enable the removal of the president and alter the role of the Constitutional Court, initiating a process that could end President Tamás Sulyok's term. Reporting emphasizes both the technical legal changes and the broader implications for checks and balances, with analysts warning of risks to constitutional safeguards. Many outlets frame the step as part of an intra-government power struggle — with Prime Minister Peter Magyar and rivals repositioning to reshape executive authority and constrain Viktor Orbán's influence. Observers say the episode is a major test of Hungary's democratic institutions and could reverberate in the EU and regional politics.

These outlets frame the move primarily as a political shake-up within Hungary: a bid to curb or reconfigure Viktor Orbán's influence, portray it as the end of an Orbán-era dominance, or present Peter Magyar's actions as a decisive power play. Reporting emphasizes partisan contestation, accusations of undermining democracy, and the wider political consequences domestically and for Hungary's international standing.
