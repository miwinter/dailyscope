Hormuz confrontation and energy security strains

Global Energy Geopolitics 2026-07-14 • 66 articles in total

In brief
  • The US is pushing to control the Strait of Hormuz through blockades and transit fees, sparking legal and diplomatic opposition.
  • Regional actors are diversifying ports and routes to reduce risk from Hormuz and protect trade flows.
  • Energy markets and infrastructure face volatility and vulnerabilities amid geopolitical tensions and ongoing shifts toward renewables.

Coverage is dominated by a new US‑Iran confrontation over the Strait of Hormuz, with US proposals to reinstate blockades, resume naval control and levy transit fees spurring diplomatic pushback and legal concerns. Markets reacted quickly: oil prices jumped and financial volatility rose as traders priced the risk of supply disruptions while shipping flows slowed and dozens of vessels were delayed. Regional and commercial actors are moving to mitigate chokepoint risk—most notably UAE port and route diversification projects—while analysts flag the broader strategic consequences for global energy routes. At the same time, attacks on refineries, climate‑driven grid stresses and cross‑border outages highlight structural vulnerabilities in energy infrastructure even as renewables and EV innovations advance long‑term supply and demand dynamics.
