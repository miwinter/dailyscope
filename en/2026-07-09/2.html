Renewed US–Iran strikes risk wider Gulf escalation

Middle East Conflict Escalation 2026-07-09 • 132 articles in total

In brief
  • Violence quickly resumed between the US and Iran after President Trump ended the ceasefire and ordered airstrikes on Iranian targets.
  • Iran claimed successful retaliatory strikes and warned of stronger responses against US forces and allies.
  • Gulf states and international agencies warned of threats to navigation, civilian harm, and urged restraint to prevent wider conflict and disruption.

Reporting across sources shows a rapid return to violence between the United States and Iran after President Trump declared the ceasefire ‘over’ and ordered fresh strikes. U.S. forces and CENTCOM reported hitting dozens of Iranian military targets—often cited as around 80–90—while Tehran and the IRGC claimed retaliatory strikes on U.S. sites and Gulf states. Gulf governments, shipping authorities and international agencies warned of disruptions to navigation, aviation and seafarers, with sirens and alerts sounding across Bahrain, Kuwait and Qatar. Analysts and humanitarian reports flagged civilian damage, infrastructure hits and the danger of miscalculation, stressing that the confrontation could widen unless diplomatic de‑escalation takes hold.
