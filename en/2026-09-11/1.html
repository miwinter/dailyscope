Bilateral deals and diplomatic shifts: investments, security pacts and ruptures

Region: Bilateral Deals 2026-09-11 • 132 articles in total

In brief
  • Canada and Ukraine formed a long-term defense and economic partnership amid broad military and security assistance.
  • Economic diplomacy drives bilateral ties through investment deals, infrastructure projects, and trade negotiations worldwide.
  • Bilateral relations fluctuate between cooperation on practical issues and conflicts over sovereignty, territory, and migration.

This collection shows a busy period of bilateral diplomacy where long-term security commitments, large investment deals and routine state visits sit alongside sharp diplomatic ruptures. Canada and Ukraine formalized an unusually long‑term defence and economic partnership while multiple partners pledged or delivered military and security assistance. Major investment and infrastructure agreements—from Portuguese credit lines for Cabo Verde to Gulf and Chinese business deals in Europe and Africa—underscore economic diplomacy as a primary tool of statecraft. At the same time, Algeria’s decision to sever ties with the UAE and several territorial or migration disputes highlight how bilateral relations can turn confrontational, even as smaller states pursue practical cooperation on fisheries, maritime security and technical assistance.
